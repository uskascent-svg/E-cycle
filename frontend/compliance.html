1. Indian legal framework

The E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 came into force on 1 April 2023 and superseded the 2016 rules. They apply to covered electrical and electronic equipment and relevant manufacturers, producers, refurbishers, dismantlers and recyclers.

Core e-waste and environmental laws

Primary framework

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 provides the parent statutory framework. The E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022 establish the current EPR and registration framework, subject to amendments and official guidance.

Related waste streams

Battery Waste Management Rules, 2022 apply separately to covered batteries. Water, Air, Hazardous and Other Wastes, Plastic Waste and Solid Waste rules may apply depending on the facility, residue, transport and processing activity.

Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)

EPR places applicable producer responsibilities around collection, recycling targets, awareness, records and returns. E-Cycle processing records must not be described as CPCB EPR certificates. Only eligible registered entities using the official system can issue or use regulatory EPR instruments.

Data protection and device data

E-Cycle handles names, contact details, addresses, accounts and potentially location data. Apply purpose limitation, consent where required, access controls, retention limits, secure sessions and safe handling of data-bearing devices. Review the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, the DPDP Rules, 2025 and applicable IT-law requirements as they commence and evolve.

4. Industry and international context

Technical and industry frameworks

R2v3, e-Stewards, ISO 14001, ISO 45001, ISO 9001, ISO/IEC 27001 and ISO 14040/14044 can provide useful operational benchmarks. They are not substitutes for statutory registration or authorisation in India.

International frameworks

The Basel Convention is the principal international framework for transboundary movements of hazardous wastes. The EU WEEE and RoHS frameworks, the EU Batteries Regulation, US RCRA and Japan's appliance-recycling laws are comparative examples, not automatically applicable Indian law.

Industry organisations and partners

Organisations such as TERI, GIZ, WEEE Forum, ISWA, MAIT and ICEA may provide research, standards or sector knowledge. Any recycler or logistics partner must be verified by legal entity, facility, category, capacity, registration and validity—marketing claims alone are not proof.
